# Professional style and guidance

These sources support readable, conventional applications. Their audience and
application rules differ; no single page count or ordering is universal.

- [LSE Careers brochure](https://info.lse.ac.uk/current-students/careers/Assets/Documents/application-and-cv-brochure.pdf),
  pp. 10–12: ordinary sans-serif type at 10–12 pt, black on white, clear sections,
  whitespace and selective bullets. Its two-page advice concerns ordinary UK
  applications; do not impose it on a detailed research CV. It normally favors
  PDF unless the employer specifies another format.
- [MIT CAPD resume checklist](https://capd.mit.edu/resources/resume-checklist/):
  conventional readable fonts, including Calibri, and selective relevant content.
  Extensive experience can justify more than one page. A one-page deliverable
  is a particular selection, not a rule for all senior applicants.
- [Oxford academic applications](https://www.ox.ac.uk/careers/careers-guidance/job-search-and-applications/writing-applications/academic-applications):
  academic applications need relevant research and publication detail. Preserve
  publication categories and distinguish published work from accepted work.
- [Greenhouse parsing guidance](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse):
  columns, layout tables, text boxes and contacts in headers/footers can impede
  parsing. Ordinary paragraphs with visible text and links are a practical
  default. Text extraction checks do not certify every ATS.

The professional sans-serif preset is intentionally simple. User choices such
as education before experience, no italics, exact section order and a phone
number are configurable preferences. Do not present them as universal research
findings. Use a skills-first variant only when the audience and evidence justify
it, rather than changing the owner's selected structure automatically.

Typography and spacing belong to a style file. Entry choice, shortening and
section order belong to a content selection. Changing the typeface must not
silently remove an entry; changing from CV to resume must not depend on a style.
